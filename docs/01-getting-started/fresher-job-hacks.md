# How Freshers Get Jobs: Tips & Hacks

As a fresher you have no "2 years of experience", but companies hire freshers every day — through campus drives, off-campus walk-ins, intern-hire conversions, and referral pipelines. Here's the playbook.

## The Fresher Reality

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "You need experience to get interviewed" | Freshers are hired for potential + fundamentals + attitude |
| "Only campus placement works" | Off-campus, internships, walk-ins, and referrals work too |
| "High CGPA = job" | CGPA helps, but projects and skills decide offers |
| "Apply to 500 jobs and pray" | 20 targeted, tailored applications beat 500 cold ones |

## The 7 Hacks That Work

### Hack 1: Internships (The Hack That Converts to Jobs)
- Internships convert at the highest rate of any fresher channel.
- Apply on [Internshala](https://internshala.com), LinkedIn, and company career pages.
- A 3-month internship is effectively a 3-month interview.
- Even unpaid/remote internships build experience + referral + a resume line.

### Hack 2: Build a Portfolio That Screams "I Can Do the Job"
- **IT/Software**: GitHub repo + deployed project + README + demo video.
- **VLSI/Embedded**: FPGA project, PCB board you built, RTOS demo (see [Specialized Domains](../../13-specialized-domains/)).
- **AI/Data Science**: Kaggle profile + published notebooks + one end-to-end ML project.
- **Drone/UAS**: build/flight-test logs, control-loop notebook.
- Recruiters search "fresher + project + domain" — make your work searchable.

### Hack 3: Off-Campus Walk-In Drives & Assessments
- Follow company career pages + Naukri/Instahyre alerts for "off-campus" drives.
- Many companies (TCS, Infosys, LTIMindtree, and dozens of semiconductor firms) run regular off-campus drives.
- Register on AMCAT/CoCubes/Unstop to get shortlists for drives.

### Hack 4: Referrals — Even as a Fresher
- Companies pay employees bonuses for referring hires — they *want* referrals.
- Ask seniors, alumni, or LinkedIn connections at target companies.
- Message pattern: *"Hi [Name], I'm a [branch] graduate from [college]. I saw you work at [Company]. I've built [project in their domain] and would really value your referral for [role]. Here's my resume. Thank you!"*
- Referral tools: ReferHub, TopReferral, LinkedIn "ask for referral".

### Hack 5: Master the Online Assessment
- Most fresher rounds start with an OA: aptitude + coding + domain MCQs.
- Freshers lose offers here more than in tech rounds.
- Practice: [08-Aptitude](../../08-Aptitude/), [09-Quantitative-Aptitude](../../09-Quantitative-Aptitude/), [25-DSA](../../25-DSA/), [17-Coding-Rounds](../../17-Coding-Rounds/).
- Do timed mock OAs — speed + accuracy decides your shortlist.

### Hack 6: Make Your Resume Fresher-Optimized
- Front-load with: **Projects → Skills → Relevant coursework → Internships**.
- No experience section is normal — put projects where experience would go.
- Add a headline: *"ECE Graduate | VLSI & Embedded Enthusiast | Built 2 FPGA projects + 1 IoT node"*.
- Quantify everything, even college work (see [Resume Tailoring](resume-tailoring)).

### Hack 7: The Follow-Up Loop
- After applying or an interview, follow up politely within 5-7 days.
- Connect with every interviewer on LinkedIn and send a short thank-you.
- Follow-ups alone recover a significant share of "no-response" applications.

```
[Headline line: Degree | Domain | Key project highlight]

[B.E./B.Tech Degree | College | Year | CGPA]
Relevant coursework: [subjects matching target jobs]

1. Project title | Tech | 1-line result
   - bullet with responsibility
   - bullet with quantified result

Languages / Tools / Domains (use JD words)

INTERNSHIPS / TRAININGS (if any)

CERTIFICATIONS
[Course / cert with year]

ACHIEVEMENTS
Hackathons won, coding ratings, hackathon badges, paper presentations
```

## Fresher-Specific Interview Prep

1. **Know your projects cold** — architecture, choices, failures, numbers. This is your "experience".
2. **Prepare fundamentals** — freshers are tested on basics, not trivia.
3. **Have an answer for salary** — fresher ranges: research the market; say a range, not a number.
4. **Ask questions at the end** — "What does training look like?" "What would success in year one look like?"
5. **Show learning ability** — freshers sell potential, so talk about what you taught yourself recently.

## Tips That Don't Work

- Applying to 500 jobs with one identical resume.
- Lying about experience — background checks catch it and kill your offer.
- Saying "I can learn anything" with nothing to show for it — bring proof (projects).
- Ignoring aptitude prep for companies that test it first.

## Checklist Before Applying

- [ ] Portfolio/GitHub live and linked everywhere
- [ ] 3-5 projects, each with a 1-line quantified impact
- [ ] 3 tailored versions of my resume (1 per target role type)
- [ ] OA practice: 10+ timed aptitude + coding mocks
- [ ] 10 referral requests sent on LinkedIn
- [ ] 20 targeted applications this week (not 100 random ones)
- [ ] Follow-up calendar set for every application
