# Job Search Strategy: How to Get Interview Calls

Your resume passing ATS is only the first hurdle. This guide covers every channel that actually generates interview calls, ranked by effectiveness, plus scripts and tracking templates.

## The Channels, Ranked by ROI

| # | Channel | Conversion vs Cold Apply | Best For |
|---|---------|--------------------------|----------|
| 1 | Referrals | 10-20x | Everyone |
| 2 | Recruiters & Agencies | 3-5x | Experienced + niche domains (VLSI, Embedded, AI) |
| 3 | Direct career-page application | 2x | Everyone |
| 4 | LinkedIn recruiter outreach | 2-4x | Active + passive candidates |
| 5 | Campus / career fairs | High for freshers | Freshers, students |
| 6 | Hackathons & competitions | High for niche | All domains |
| 7 | Job portals (cold) | 1x baseline | Everyone |
| 8 | Cold email to hiring managers | 1-2x | Small/medium companies |

## 1. Referrals (Highest ROI)

- Search `"CompanyName" site:linkedin.com/in` to find current employees.
- Prefer people 1-2 years in the company (more responsive) in your domain team.
- Use referral platforms: ReferHub, TopReferral, TripHunter, and LinkedIn "Ask for a referral" on the job posting.
- **Referral request script:**
  > Hi [Name], I hope you're doing well. I'm a [role] engineer with [X years] experience in [domain]. I'm applying for the [Role] position at [Company] and would be grateful if you'd consider referring me. Here's my resume and a 2-line summary. Happy to answer any questions. Thanks!

## 2. Recruiters & Staffing Agencies

- Connect with 3-5 recruiters who specialize in your domain on LinkedIn.
- Message them with: current role, target role, years of experience, notice period, CTC expectations, location, and your resume.
- Agencies often get roles before they're posted publicly — use them for early access.
- **Warning:** never pay a fee, never share OTPs, and always verify the agency (see [Recruitment Agencies](recruitment-agencies)).

## 3. Direct Career-Page Application

- Apply on the official career page first — it lands in the company's primary ATS.
- Then mirror the application on LinkedIn/other portals for visibility.
- Apply within 48 hours of posting; early applicants get priority review.
- Tailor your resume keywords per job description (see [Resume & ATS](resume-ats)).

## 4. LinkedIn Recruiter Outreach

- Keep your profile 100% complete and toggle **Open to Work** (visible to recruiters only, or public).
- Post weekly about your projects, domain insights, or learnings to stay on recruiter feeds.
- Search for "Recruiter [domain]" or "Talent Acquisition [domain]" and send short, non-spammy connection notes.
- Refresh your profile every few days — LinkedIn boosts recently-active profiles in recruiter searches.

## 5. Campus / Career Fairs

- Keep university placement-cell profiles updated and registered on **Unstop**, **HackerEarth**, **Dare2Compete**, **Amcat/CoCubes**.
- Many VLSI/electronics/embedded companies hire through off-campus walk-in drives — follow their careers pages.
- Attend virtual career fairs on platforms like **CareerCon**, **GradConnect**, and company-hosted events.

## 6. Hackathons & Competitions

- Domain hackathons (IoT, drone, embedded, AI/DS) put winners directly on recruiter shortlists.
- Platforms: Devpost, HackerEarth, Unstop, IEEE, GitHub Campus Experts, and company hackathons (e.g., Smart India Hackathon).
- Winning a hackathon is often equivalent to a strong referral for startups and R&D teams.

## 7. Cold Outreach to Hiring Managers

- Find engineering leads / hiring managers on LinkedIn.
- Send a 3-4 sentence message: who you are, one key achievement, why this company, resume link.
- Follow up once after 5-7 days. Never spam.

## Rules of the Game

1. **Apply fast** — within 48 hours of posting.
2. **Tailor every application** — keyword-match the job description.
3. **Follow up** — one polite nudge per week is fine.
4. **Never ghost a recruiter** — respond promptly to every message.
5. **Track everything** — you cannot manage what you don't measure.
